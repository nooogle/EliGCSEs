# Worlds and Lives — Poetry Comparison Matrix

This matrix helps match the 15 "Worlds and Lives" poems by theme. In the exam, you will be given one named poem and you must choose a second one to compare it to. Knowing how the poems share themes is essential for picking a strong pairing.

---

## The "Worlds and Lives" Clusters & Pairings

| Theme | Best Poem Pairings | Key Comparison Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature, Belonging & Place** | *Lines Written in Early Spring*<br>*Shall earth no more inspire thee*<br>*In a London Drawingroom*<br>*With Birds You're Never Lonely* | Wordsworth and Brontë find spiritual sustenance in nature; Eliot shows the city as its opposite — oppressive and life-denying. |
| **Identity, Heritage & Diaspora** | *Homing*<br>*Name Journeys*<br>*pot*<br>*A Portable Paradise*<br>*The Jewellery Maker* | Exploring accent/dialect (Homing), names (Name Journeys), dual identity (pot), and carrying home internally (A Portable Paradise, The Jewellery Maker). |
| **Oppression, Power & Protest** | *England in 1819*<br>*A Century Later*<br>*Thirteen* | Shelley's anger at the corrupt monarchy vs. Femi's modern examination of systemic racism and police targeting of Black youth. |
| **Generational Trauma & Heritage** | *Like an Heiress*<br>*A Wider View*<br>*The Jewellery Maker*<br>*On an Afternoon Train* | How the past shapes the present — inheriting historical memory, navigating racial memory, and craft passed through generations. |

## Quick-Glance Poem Summaries

1. **Lines Written in Early Spring** (William Wordsworth)
   - *Vibe:* Romantic. Nature is beautiful and joyful; humanity is destroying itself.
   - *Quote:* "And much it grieved my heart to think / What man has made of man."

2. **England in 1819** (Percy Bysshe Shelley)
   - *Vibe:* Angry sonnet attacking the corrupt royals and military.
   - *Quote:* "A people starved and stabbed in the untilled field"

3. **Shall earth no more inspire thee** (Emily Brontë)
   - *Vibe:* Nature (the Moors) as spiritual home and creative force; a dialogue between the earth and a poet who has abandoned it.
   - *Quote:* "Thy mind is ever moving / In regions dark to thee"

4. **In a London Drawingroom** (George Eliot)
   - *Vibe:* Bleak critique of industrial London; life feels boxed in and joyless.
   - *Quote:* "No bird can make a shadow as it flies"

5. **On an Afternoon Train from Purley to Victoria, 1955** (James Berry)
   - *Vibe:* A quiet, tense encounter reflecting experiences of racial exclusion (Windrush generation).
   - *Quote:* "I stared at the empty seat / beside me..."

6. **Name Journeys** (Raman Mundair)
   - *Vibe:* A name carries the weight of culture, identity, and the pain of forced assimilation.
   - *Quote:* "A mispronounced word / a clumsy attempt"

7. **pot** (Shamshad Khan)
   - *Vibe:* Exploring dual identity — feeling like a vessel carrying multiple cultures simultaneously.
   - *Quote:* "i am a pot / i'm not a pot"

8. **A Wider View** (Seni Seneviratne)
   - *Vibe:* Reconciling different strands of heritage and mixed identity; searching for a wider, fuller picture of the self.

9. **Homing** (Liz Berry)
   - *Vibe:* Celebrating the Black Country dialect as a form of belonging — reclaiming a voice that was suppressed.
   - *Quote:* "the vowels / hauling me back"

10. **A Century Later** (Imtiaz Dharker)
    - *Vibe:* Schoolgirls defying terror and male oppression to gain an education (strongly linked to Malala Yousafzai).
    - *Quote:* "The schoolbell is a call to battle"

11. **The Jewellery Maker** (Louisa Adjoa Parker)
    - *Vibe:* Heritage and identity passed down through craft and hands; the act of making as a form of cultural memory and connection across generations.

12. **With Birds You're Never Lonely** (Raymond Antrobus)
    - *Vibe:* Finding connection and solace in nature and birdsong amid urban isolation and personal grief.

13. **A Portable Paradise** (Roger Robinson)
    - *Vibe:* Carrying peace and hope internally when the outside world is hostile; paradise as memory of a person, not a place.
    - *Quote:* "if I speak of Paradise, / then I'm speaking of my grandmother"

14. **Like an Heiress** (Grace Nichols)
    - *Vibe:* Claiming the richness of a Caribbean heritage and identity, flipping the traditional English idea of what an "heiress" inherits.

15. **Thirteen** (Caleb Femi)
    - *Vibe:* A young Black boy stopped and searched by police — the moment innocence is stripped away by systemic racism.
    - *Quote:* "You are a boy... you are a threat"

## Preparation Tip

You cannot memorise all 15 poems perfectly. Pick 4 or 5 "Super Poems" that cover multiple themes (e.g., *Thirteen* for oppression, *Homing* for identity/belonging, *Lines Written in Early Spring* for nature/humanity, *In a London Drawingroom* for city/modern life). Learn 3–4 excellent quotes for each of these Super Poems.
