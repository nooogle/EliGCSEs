# Worlds and Lives — Poetry Comparison Matrix

This matrix helps match the 15 "Worlds and Lives" poems by theme. In the exam, you will be given one named poem and you must choose a second one to compare it to. Knowing how the poems share themes is essential for picking a strong pairing.

---

## The "Worlds and Lives" Clusters & Pairings

| Theme | Best Poem Pairings | Key Comparison Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature vs. Modern/City Life** | *Lines Written in Early Spring*<br>*In a London Drawingroom*<br>*With Birds You're Never Lonely* | Wordsworth finds joy in nature but despairs at what humanity has done. Eliot presents the city as oppressive and restrictive. |
| **Place, Identity & Belonging** | *Homing*<br>*Name Journeys*<br>*pot*<br>*A Portable Paradise* | Exploring accent/dialect (Homing), names (Name Journeys), and reclaiming identity when separated from a physical homeland. |
| **Oppression, Power & Protest** | *England in 1819*<br>*A Century Later*<br>*Thirteen* | Shelley's anger at the corrupt monarchy vs. Femi's modern examination of systemic racism/police targeting of Black youth. |
| **Generational Trauma & Heritage** | *Like an Heiress*<br>*A Wider View*<br>*pot* | How the past shapes the present. Inheriting historical memory across generations. |

## Quick-Glance Poem Summaries

1. **Lines Written in Early Spring** (William Wordsworth)
   - *Vibe:* Romantic. Nature is beautiful; humanity is ruining itself.
   - *Quote:* "And much it grieved my heart to think / What man has made of man."

2. **England in 1819** (Percy Bysshe Shelley)
   - *Vibe:* Angry sonic sonnet attacking the corrupt royals and military.
   - *Quote:* "A people starved and stabbed in the untilled field"

3. **In a London Drawingroom** (George Eliot)
   - *Vibe:* Bleak critique of industrial London; life feels boxed in.
   - *Quote:* "No bird can make a shadow as it flies"

4. **On an Afternoon Train from Purley to Victoria, 1955** (James Berry)
   - *Vibe:* A quiet, tense encounter reflecting experiences of immigration (Windrush generation).
   - *Quote:* "I stared at the empty seat / beside me..."

5. **Name Journeys** (Raman Mundair)
   - *Vibe:* How a name carries the weight of culture, identity, and the pain of assimilation.
   - *Quote:* "A mispronounced word / a clumsy attempt"

6. **pot** (Shamshad Khan)
   - *Vibe:* Exploring dual identity, feeling like a vessel carrying multiple cultures.
   - *Quote:* "i am a pot / i'm not a pot"

7. **A Century Later** (Imtiaz Dharker)
   - *Vibe:* Schoolgirls defying terror and male oppression to gain an education (often linked to Malala).
   - *Quote:* "The schoolbell is a call to battle"

8. **The Right Word** (Imtiaz Dharker)
   - *Vibe:* How language shapes our perception of others — enemy or child?
   - *Quote:* "Outside the door, / lurking in the shadows, / is a terrorist."

9. **A Portable Paradise** (Roger Robinson)
   - *Vibe:* Carrying peace and hope internally when the outside world is hostile.
   - *Quote:* "if I speak of Paradise, / then I'm speaking of my grandmother"

10. **Thirteen** (Caleb Femi)
    - *Vibe:* The reality of being a young Black boy stopped and searched by police. Loss of innocence.
    - *Quote:* "You are a boy... you are a threat"

11. **Homing** (Liz Berry)
    - *Vibe:* Celebrating regional dialects (Black Country) and holding onto a vocal 'home'.
    - *Quote:* "the vowels / hauling me back"

12. **A Wider View** (Seni Seneviratne)
    - *Vibe:* Reconciling different heritage strands, exploring history.

13. **Like an Heiress** (Grace Nichols)
    - *Vibe:* Claiming the richness of a Caribbean heritage, flipping the traditional English idea of an "heiress".

14. **With Birds You're Never Lonely** (Raymond Antrobus)
    - *Vibe:* Finding connection and solace in nature amidst urban or personal isolation.

## Preparation Tip

You cannot memorise all 15 poems perfectly. Pick 4 or 5 "Super Poems" that cover multiple themes (e.g., *Thirteen* for oppression, *Homing* for identity/belonging, *Lines Written in Early Spring* for nature/humanity, *In a London Drawingroom* for city/modern life). Learn 3–4 excellent quotes for each of these Super Poems.
