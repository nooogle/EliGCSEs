Paper 2A — Thematic Studies — Christianity & Sikhism
Paper 2A (8062/2A): 105 minutes · 96 marks + 3 SPaG · Closed book · You answer questions on all your studied themes. Approximately 4 themes, ~26 minutes each. No extract provided — all from memory.

Question Structure (per theme)

PartMarksWhat to doTime
(a)1Name or define a key term1 min
(b)1Brief factual statement about a belief/practice1 min
(c)4Explain a religious view with two pieces of evidence4 min
(d)6Explain two contrasting religious views with evidence6 min
(e)12+3 SPaGEvaluate a statement — argue for and against using religious teachings, plus a clear conclusion13 min
Total per theme: 24 marks. Four themes × 24 = 96 marks. SPaG (up to 3 marks) is awarded on one of the 12-mark questions — write clearly throughout.

How to Answer Each Part

Part (c) — 4 Marks

Point 1
State a religious teaching or belief → cite specific evidence (named scripture, church teaching, Guru teaching) → explain how this leads to the view on the issue
Point 2
A second religious argument with its own evidence and explanation. Must be genuinely different from Point 1.

Generic moral arguments without religious references score Level 1 (1–2 marks) only.

Part (d) — 6 Marks — Contrasting Views

View 1
State the religious position clearly → cite named evidence (scripture, authority, teaching) → explain why this is a valid religious response
View 2
A genuinely contrasting position with its own evidence and explanation. Examples of genuine contrast: Catholic vs. liberal Christian; traditional Sikh vs. progressive interpretation; Christian vs. Sikh. Views must be meaningfully different — not the same view rephrased.

Part (e) — 12 Marks — Evaluation

FOR ×2
Two developed arguments supporting the statement, each with named religious evidence and a chain of reasoning (not just a listed point)
AGAINST ×2
Two developed arguments opposing the statement, each from a contrasting religious perspective with specific evidence
Conclusion
Your reasoned judgement — weigh both sides, state which is more convincing and why, link back to the exact wording of the statement

Level 4 (10–12): Multiple developed arguments both sides · named religious teachings · contrasting viewpoints (e.g., Catholic vs. liberal, Christian vs. Sikh) · clear evaluative conclusion · good SPaG throughout.

Essential language: “This suggests that...” · “However, one could argue...” · “In contrast...” · “According to the Bible/Guru Granth Sahib...” · “The Catholic Church holds that...” · “Guru Nanak taught...” · “On balance...” · “Ultimately, this statement is [partially] correct because...”

Theme A — Relationships & Families

Christianity
Sex & marriage: sex belongs in marriage only; body is ‘a temple of the Holy Spirit’ (1 Cor 6:19); ‘they become one flesh’ (Genesis 2:24).

Contraception: Catholic = opposes artificial (Humanae Vitae 1968); Protestant = generally accepts it.

Same-sex: traditional = opposed (Lev 18:22); liberal = ‘love your neighbour’ (Matt 22:39); C of E divided.

Divorce: Catholic = no divorce, only annulment; ‘What God has joined, let no one separate’ (Matt 19:6); Protestant = allows as last resort.
Sikhism
Marriage: Anand Karaj (‘ceremony of bliss’) — four lavan around the Guru Granth Sahib = four stages of spiritual union.

Contraception: No strict prohibition; life is sacred but responsible parenthood is valued.

Same-sex: Sikh Rehat Maryada = marriage between man and woman; SGPC does not permit same-sex marriages in Gurdwaras.

Gender equality: ‘So why call her inferior? From her, kings are born’ — Guru Nanak. Women lead prayers, read Guru Granth Sahib. Langar = everyone equal.

Theme B — Religion & Life

Christianity
Sanctity of life: ‘You knitted me together in my mother’s womb’ (Psalm 139:13); life = sacred gift from God.

Abortion: Catholic = always wrong (Psalm 139:13); Protestant/C of E = ‘lesser evil’ in limited cases (rape, risk to mother, severe abnormality).

Euthanasia: Catholic = always wrong; palliative care = Christian alternative; ‘life to the full’ (John 10:10); some liberal Christians accept dying with dignity.

Environment: stewardship (Genesis 2:15); ‘the earth is the Lord’s’ (Psalm 24:1); Christian Aid campaigns for climate justice.
Sikhism
Sanctity of life: life is given by Waheguru; follows Hukam (God’s will); all life is sacred.

Abortion: generally discouraged (interferes with Hukam); accepts in exceptional cases; no absolute prohibition.

Euthanasia: opposed — Hukam determines when life ends; Naam Japna (meditation) and Sangat (community) support the dying; Seva = caring for the dying.

Environment: ‘Air is the Guru, Water is the Father, Earth is the Great Mother’ (Guru Granth Sahib); Sewa extends to caring for creation.

Theme C — The Existence of God

Christianity
Design Argument (Paley): complex watch → watchmaker; complex universe → God. Criticism: Darwin (evolution without designer). Counter: evolution may be God’s method.

First Cause (Aquinas): everything has a cause; no infinite regress; ‘uncaused cause’ = God. Criticism: why can’t the universe be uncaused?

Problem of Evil: free will; soul-making (Irenaeus); God’s greater purpose (Job); evil = absence of good (Augustine).

Revelation: general = nature/reason; special = Bible; Jesus = ultimate special revelation.
Sikhism
God (Waheguru): Ik Onkar (One God); Mool Mantar: ‘There is One God; Truth is His Name; He is the Creator.’ Formless, eternal, immanent and transcendent.

Knowing God: not through reason alone — through devotion (Bhakti) and living by the Guru’s teachings.

Problem of Evil: Hukam (God’s will/divine order) — suffering is part of God’s plan; meditation on Waheguru (Naam Japna) brings peace amid suffering; haumai (ego) causes human-made evil.

Theme D — Peace & Conflict

Christianity
Just War Theory (Augustine/Aquinas) — all 6 must be met: just cause · legitimate authority · right intention · last resort · proportionality · reasonable chance of success.

Pacifism (Quakers): ‘Love your enemies’ (Matt 5:44); ‘those who live by the sword die by the sword’ (Matt 26:52).

Peace: ‘Blessed are the peacemakers’ (Matt 5:9).

Forgiveness: ‘Father, forgive them’ (Luke 23:34); Corrymeela Community = reconciliation.

Nuclear: fail proportionality in Just War; CND has Christian support.
Sikhism
Dharam Yudh (righteous war) — permitted only as last resort to defend the innocent; just cause; protect civilians; no looting.

Khalsa: formed by Guru Gobind Singh to defend the oppressed and protect religious freedom.

Kirpan: one of the 5 Ks — small ceremonial dagger symbolising duty to protect the innocent.

Forgiveness: ‘Forgiveness is the most powerful weapon’ (Guru Granth Sahib); forgive personal wrongs but resist systemic injustice.

Terrorism: directly contradicts Seva; Dharam Yudh must always protect civilians.

Theme E — Crime & Punishment

Christianity
4 aims: retribution · deterrence · reformation · protection. Christianity prioritises reformation.

Retribution challenged: ‘eye for an eye’ (Exodus 21:24) → overturned by Jesus: ‘turn the other cheek’ (Matt 5:38-39).

Forgiveness: Luke 23:34; ‘let he who is without sin cast the first stone’ (John 8:7); forgive 70×7 (Matt 18:22); Prodigal Son.

Capital punishment: Catholic now opposes (2018); ‘vengeance is mine’ (Rom 12:19).

Prison: ‘I was in prison and you came to visit me’ (Matt 25:36); Prison Fellowship; imago Dei (dignity of all).
Sikhism
Justice (Nyaya) & mercy: both valued; crime harms the Sangat and must be addressed.

Capital punishment: generally opposed — only Waheguru has the right to take life.

Rehabilitation: Seva (community service) as a form of reform; the reformed person is welcomed back by the Sangat.

Forgiveness: encouraged for personal wrongs; systemic injustice must be actively resisted to protect the innocent.

Theme F — Human Rights & Social Justice

Christianity
Equality: imago Dei (Genesis 1:27) — all humans made in God’s image; ‘neither Jew nor Gentile, slave nor free’ (Galatians 3:28); Good Samaritan = compassion crosses all boundaries.

Wealth: ‘Cannot serve God and money’ (Matt 6:24); Rich Man & Lazarus (Luke 16); ‘love of money = root of evil’ (1 Tim 6:10).

Poverty: ‘I was hungry and you gave me food’ (Matt 25:35); Christian Aid / CAFOD / Tearfund; Liberation Theology — ‘preferential option for the poor.’
Sikhism
Equality: Guru Nanak abolished caste; ‘God has no caste’; ‘Why call her inferior?’; Langar = all eat together; Khalsa open to all.

3 pillars: Naam Japna (devotion) · Kirat Karna (honest work) · Vand Chhakna (share with others).

Charity: Daswandh = give 10% of income; Khalsa Aid operates globally (food relief regardless of faith); ‘cursed is money earned through exploitation’ (Guru Granth Sahib).

Women: spiritually equal; women lead prayers and read Guru Granth Sahib.
