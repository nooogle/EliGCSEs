Section A — Reading

Q1
Select Four Statements
Multiple Choice
4

You are given a list of statements about the text (often 8–10 options). Select exactly four that are correct according to the extract.

  • Read the relevant section of the text first, then read the options
  • Eliminate obvious wrong answers — select only what the text directly supports
  • Do not select more than four — you lose marks for wrong answers
  • Takes 5 minutes maximum — do not overthink
Q2
Language Analysis
Specific lines given
8

Analyse how the writer uses language in a named section of the text (usually lines 1–14 or similar). Write 2–3 focused paragraphs.

Each paragraph: Select a precise quotation → Identify a technique (simile, metaphor, alliteration, personification, short sentence, etc.) → Explain the meaning → Analyse the effect on the reader.

Never just identify a technique. Every point must explain what it does to the reader.

suggests... implies... conveys a sense of... creates the impression that... emphasises... makes the reader feel... the word [X] is particularly effective because...
Vary your techniques across the 2–3 paragraphs. Do not write three paragraphs about metaphors. Re-read the question to check what aspect of language you are focusing on.
Q3
Structure Analysis
Whole text — one effect
8

2026 change: Q3 now asks you to focus on ONE specific structural effect — how the writer builds that effect across the whole text. Do not list multiple structural features.

  1. Identify the one structural effect specified in the question (e.g., building tension, creating sympathy, establishing mystery)
  2. Show evidence from different points in the text — beginning, middle, end — to show how it develops
  3. Explain the cumulative effect on the reader as the effect builds

Use structural vocabulary: foreshadowing · flashback · in medias res · cyclical structure · pace (fast/slow) · perspective shift · climax · cliffhanger · motif

Q4
Evaluation — the big one
Specific lines given
20

A statement about the text is given (usually about a specific section from roughly line 19 onward). You are asked whether you agree or disagree. Write 3 developed paragraphs.

Structure of each paragraph:

  1. State your view (agree / partly agree / disagree with the statement)
  2. Quote precisely from the relevant section
  3. Identify a technique the writer uses
  4. Analyse the effect — what does the technique achieve?
  5. Evaluate — how successfully does the writer achieve what the statement claims? Link back to the statement explicitly
The writer successfully... however, one could argue... this is particularly effective because... the statement is [partially] accurate because... the writer achieves this by... I agree/disagree that...
This is 25% of your reading marks. Plan for 2 minutes before writing. Aim for 3 paragraphs — not 5 short ones. Every paragraph must link back to the exact wording of the statement.

Section B — Writing

Q5
Creative Writing
Choice of two prompts
40

You choose one of two options: (A) descriptive writing inspired by a photograph; (B) opening of a story with a given title or sentence. Narrative writing (Option B) generally scores higher with AQA — choose it unless descriptive writing is your clear strength.

The 50-Minute Plan

Minutes 1–5: Plan
  • Decide: setting, protagonist, the central tension or event, and how it ends
  • Note 3–4 vivid details or images you want to use
  • Plan your structure: hook opening → build situation → tension/revelation → ending
Minutes 6–45: Write
  • Aim for 500–700 words
  • Open with impact: in medias res, a striking image, or a compelling voice
  • Vary sentence length deliberately: short for tension, long for atmosphere
  • Use specific nouns and strong verbs over vague adjective-heavy description
  • Use at least 2–3 techniques: pathetic fallacy, simile, personification, metaphor
  • Show character through action and dialogue, not just description
Minutes 46–50: Check SPaG
  • SPaG is 16 of 40 marks — this check is not optional
  • Read through once for spelling; once for punctuation (commas, full stops, speech marks)
  • Check apostrophes in contractions and possessives
  • Do not cross out large sections — neatly correct individual words
AQA rewards ambitious vocabulary even if there are occasional errors — write like a writer, not like someone playing it safe. Use varied sentence openers (not always ‘The’ or ‘She’). Let the reader feel something.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Q1: Selecting more than four statements — check your count
  • Q2: Identifying techniques without explaining their effect on the reader
  • Q3: Listing multiple structural features instead of tracking ONE effect across the text
  • Q4: Writing five short paragraphs instead of three developed ones; forgetting to link back to the statement in every paragraph
  • Q5: Starting with backstory instead of action; using only adjectives with no technique; not checking SPaG (16 marks at stake)
