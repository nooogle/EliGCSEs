Paper 1 — Shakespeare and the 19th-Century Novel

Section A: Shakespeare

  • One question on the chosen play
  • An extract is printed; analyse it and write about the play as a whole
  • 30 marks (+ 4 marks AO4 on one question per paper)
  • Eli's text: Macbeth

Section B: 19th-Century Novel

  • One question on the chosen novel
  • Same structure: extract analysis + whole-text discussion
  • 30 marks
  • Eli's text: A Christmas Carol, Charles Dickens

Paper 2 — Modern Texts and Poetry

Section A: Modern Text

  • Choice of two questions; answer one
  • Essay-style — no extract provided
  • 30 marks (+ 4 marks AO4)
  • Eli's text: An Inspector Calls, J.B. Priestley

Section B: Poetry Anthology — Comparative

  • One named poem from the cluster + one of student's choice from the same cluster
  • 30 marks
  • Eli's cluster: Worlds and Lives

Section C: Unseen Poetry

  • Question 1: respond to one unseen poem — 24 marks
  • Question 2: compare it to a second unseen poem — 8 marks

Assessment Objectives

AO2 is the biggest mark-earner (42.5%). Precise language analysis with terminology is essential. AO3 context is only 15% — weave it in, never front-load it.
AODescriptionWeighting
AO1Read, understand and respond; maintain a critical style; use quotations to support interpretations37.5%
AO2Analyse language, form and structure; use appropriate subject terminology42.5%
AO3Understand how texts relate to their historical and cultural contexts15%
AO4Vocabulary, sentence structure, spelling and punctuation5%

What Examiners Want

  • AO1: A personal, critical argument. Quotes embedded naturally — not just dropped in.
  • AO2: Analyse the effect of specific language/structural choices — not just identify techniques.
  • AO3: Context integrated into analysis, not listed as background. Show how context shapes the text.
  • Avoid plot retelling. Examiners reward interpretation, not summary.
