All 15 Poems

1
Lines Written in Early Spring
William Wordsworth
Romantic. Nature is beautiful and joyful; humanity is destroying itself and its connection to the natural world.
"And much it grieved my heart to think / What man has made of man."
2
England in 1819
Percy Bysshe Shelley
Angry Petrarchan sonnet attacking corrupt royals and military. Political protest as poetic form.
"A people starved and stabbed in the untilled field"
3
Shall earth no more inspire thee
Emily Brontë
Nature (the Moors) as spiritual home and creative force. A dialogue between the earth and a poet who has withdrawn from it.
"Thy mind is ever moving / In regions dark to thee"
4
In a London Drawingroom
George Eliot
Bleak critique of industrial London. Life feels boxed in, joyless, and cut off from nature.
"No bird can make a shadow as it flies"
5
On an Afternoon Train from Purley to Victoria, 1955
James Berry
A quiet, tense encounter reflecting racial exclusion (Windrush generation). Isolation in plain sight.
"I stared at the empty seat / beside me..."
6
Name Journeys
Raman Mundair
A name carries the weight of culture, identity, and the pain of forced assimilation.
"A mispronounced word / a clumsy attempt"
7
pot
Shamshad Khan
Dual identity — feeling like a vessel carrying multiple cultures simultaneously. Form mirrors content.
"i am a pot / i'm not a pot"
8
A Wider View
Seni Seneviratne
Reconciling different strands of heritage and mixed identity. Searching for a fuller, wider picture of the self.
9
Homing
Liz Berry
Celebrating the Black Country dialect as a form of belonging — reclaiming a voice that was suppressed.
"the vowels / hauling me back"
10
A Century Later
Imtiaz Dharker
Schoolgirls defying terror and male oppression to gain an education (strongly linked to Malala Yousafzai).
"The schoolbell is a call to battle"
11
The Jewellery Maker
Louisa Adjoa Parker
Heritage and identity passed down through craft and hands. Making as cultural memory and connection across generations.
12
With Birds You're Never Lonely
Raymond Antrobus
Finding connection and solace in nature and birdsong amid urban isolation and personal grief.
13
A Portable Paradise
Roger Robinson
Carrying peace and hope internally when the outside world is hostile. Paradise as memory of a person, not a place.
"if I speak of Paradise, / then I'm speaking of my grandmother"
14
Like an Heiress
Grace Nichols
Claiming the richness of Caribbean heritage, flipping the traditional English idea of what an "heiress" inherits.
15
Thirteen
Caleb Femi
A young Black boy stopped and searched by police — the moment innocence is stripped away by systemic racism.
"You are a boy... you are a threat"

Preparation Tip

You cannot memorise all 15 poems perfectly. Pick 4 or 5 "Super Poems" that cover multiple themes — e.g. Thirteen for oppression, Homing for identity/belonging, Lines Written in Early Spring for nature/humanity, In a London Drawingroom for city/modern life. Learn 3–4 excellent quotes for each Super Poem and know the key techniques and context for each one.
