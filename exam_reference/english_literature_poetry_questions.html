POETRY
PRACTICE
Worlds and Lives — comparative questions
Paper 2, Section B — 30 marks · ~30 minutes · Closed book · You must quote from memory.

How the Question Works

AQA names one poem in the question. You choose one other poem from the Worlds and Lives cluster to compare it with.

"Compare how poets present [theme] in [named poem] and one other poem from the anthology."

Choose the second poem where you can make the most interesting comparison — not the most obvious match, but the one you know best and can quote accurately.

Answer Structure (30 minutes)

ParagraphWhat to do
IntroName both poems and briefly state how each treats the theme differently
Para 1Close analysis of a quote from the named poem — language, structure, effect (AO2). Weave in relevant context (AO3)
Para 2Close analysis of a quote from your chosen poem. Link back with a comparison word
Para 3A second point of comparison — zoom into a different quote from each poem
ConclusionSummarise the key similarity and difference; comment on overall effect
Key comparison words: similarly / in contrast / whereas / both poets / unlike / Femi echoes Dharker's... / where Robinson focuses on... Wordsworth instead...

Remember: AO2 is 42.5% of marks. Don't just identify techniques — always explain the effect on the reader.

Practice Questions

Q1
Compare how poets present feelings of belonging in 'Homing' by Liz Berry and one other poem from the anthology.
Suggested pairings: 'A Portable Paradise' · 'Name Journeys' · 'Lines Written in Early Spring'
Q2
Compare how poets present the relationship between people and nature in 'Lines Written in Early Spring' by William Wordsworth and one other poem from the anthology.
Suggested pairings: 'With Birds You're Never Lonely' · 'Shall earth no more inspire thee' · 'Like an Heiress'
Q3
Compare how poets present the effects of injustice in 'Thirteen' by Caleb Femi and one other poem from the anthology.
Suggested pairings: 'A Century Later' · 'England in 1819' · 'pot'
Q4
Compare how poets present cultural heritage and identity in 'pot' by Shamshad Khan and one other poem from the anthology.
Suggested pairings: 'Name Journeys' · 'A Wider View' · 'Like an Heiress'
Q5
Compare how poets present the experience of living in a city in 'In a London Drawingroom' by George Eliot and one other poem from the anthology.
Suggested pairings: 'With Birds You're Never Lonely' · 'On an Afternoon Train from Purley to Victoria, 1955' · 'England in 1819'
Q6
Compare how poets present resilience and hope in 'A Portable Paradise' by Roger Robinson and one other poem from the anthology.
Suggested pairings: 'A Century Later' · 'Homing' · 'Lines Written in Early Spring'
Q7
Compare how poets present the importance of language and communication in 'Name Journeys' by Raman Mundair and one other poem from the anthology.
Suggested pairings: 'Homing' · 'With Birds You're Never Lonely' · 'On an Afternoon Train from Purley to Victoria, 1955'
Q8
Compare how poets present the effects of the past on the present in 'A Wider View' by Seni Seneviratne and one other poem from the anthology.
Suggested pairings: 'Like an Heiress' · 'A Century Later' · 'pot'
Q9
Compare how poets present political anger or protest in 'England in 1819' by Percy Bysshe Shelley and one other poem from the anthology.
Suggested pairings: 'Thirteen' · 'A Century Later' · 'In a London Drawingroom'
Q10
Compare how poets present encounters between people of different backgrounds in 'On an Afternoon Train from Purley to Victoria, 1955' by James Berry and one other poem from the anthology.
Suggested pairings: 'Thirteen' · 'Name Journeys' · 'pot'
